## Measurement and inference caveats

- The proposal is strongest when the motif is an exact-bin sinusoid over the 2 s section. With 48,000 Hz and 96,000 samples, DFT-bin spacing is 0.5 Hz. A projection at a declared bin gives a reproducible component-amplitude proxy. If the motif is not bin-centered or has a changing phase/envelope, leakage and window choice matter; use a predeclared common window/estimator, or a predeclared narrow band, identically in both sections. Do not choose bins after seeing which produces >=6 dB.
- “Relative energy” needs an explicit denominator. A 6 dB power ratio means 4:1 (`10 log10`); a 6 dB amplitude ratio means 2:1 (`20 log10`). These are equivalent only when power is proportional to squared amplitude under consistent normalization. Name the metric as power or amplitude and use the matching formula. A ratio with a zero or floor-dominated denominator is not meaningful; declare a floor and require both section estimates above it, or use a bounded normalized contrast such as `10 log10((P+floor)/(Q+floor))` with the floor fixed in advance and report raw powers as well. Avoid an implicit epsilon that makes silence look like a huge contrast.
- A single frequency-component change measures one analytic structural property, not motif identity, harmonic role, phrase development, musicality, or audible quality. Overall level normalization, unrelated spectral energy, and phase can affect some estimators. Report the named component's estimates and the surrounding estimator definition, not just a dB headline.
- A 4 s loop's endpoint and adjacent-slope values are local numerical checks only. They do not show continuity at a runtime loop point after codec conversion, correct playback, or a perceptually smooth seam. Existing global centroid/bandwidth values likewise do not prove time-varying structure.
- The 6 dB target is a round-local analytic criterion, not a general musicality, audibility, mastering, or production standard. No borrowed samples, engine behavior, canon, perceptual, rights, or listening claim is made here.
