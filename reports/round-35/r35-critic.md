# Round 35 critic — duration/centroid proxy falsification

## Scope and evidence read

I read `.shokunin/audio/LOG.md` (round-35 declaration), `corpus/fixtures/measure_audio.py`, `corpus/fixtures/build_artifacts.py`, the fixture-local threshold note, and the existing normal receipt `corpus/live/runs/20261013T032603527866Z-normal/measurements.json`. I made no shell calls, ran no commands, and made no fit attempt; therefore there are no shell command/exit pairs or setup/fit failures to report. This is a numeric-proxy critique, not an audition.

The declared movement baseline receipt reports duration/centroid: `movement` .24 s / 11934.484 Hz; `movement_alt` .36 s / 12024.215 Hz. `movement_alt` already passes the generic rule by duration delta .12 s although centroid relative delta is only .007519. The checker defines each variant as passing if duration delta is at least .020 s **OR** relative centroid delta is at least .10. The declared third target .30 s is .06 s from each duration, so it clears the duration arm against both baselines before its spectrum is considered.

## Falsification / limits

- Duration measures file length, not signal content. Two files can have identical sample count and duration while containing byte-identical PCM (or differ only in a sample, phase, ordering, or an inaudible-scale perturbation). Conversely, padding or trimming silence changes duration without changing the active event. Thus a duration-OR pass is not evidence of distinct signal content, let alone perceived distinctness. Here the proposed target's .06 s offset alone satisfies both duration comparisons.
- The centroid in `measure()` is a single magnitude-weighted mean of the whole-file real FFT. It discards phase and temporal ordering and compresses the entire magnitude spectrum into one scalar; many different spectra/signals share the same mean, while small spectral mass at high frequencies can move it substantially. It cannot establish that two sources are spectrally or perceptually distinct. This is particularly relevant to the movement baseline: the recorded centroid changes by only ~0.75% despite the .12 s duration difference. No criterion checks bandwidth, spectrum shape, transient timing, or similarity.
- The proposed requirement that centroid differ by >=10% from **each** movement is an extra *candidate-selection filter*, not validation that passing candidates are distinct. It can reject useful candidates whose centroid lies near either reference even when they differ in duration by >=.020 s; the OR pass rule itself would still call them distinct. Conversely a centroid-only pass can be driven by a narrow high-frequency component while most of the waveform remains a clone. Requiring this centroid filter in addition to duration effectively changes the selection policy from the generic OR and should be described as such, not as stronger proof.
- A whole-file centroid is affected by duration/windowing and the relative energy of components, so the same underlying event with appended silence or changed tail weighting can shift the statistic. The note/code do not estimate uncertainty or establish a population-level separation margin. Two comparisons against related fixtures are not independent samples or statistical calibration.

## Conclusion

At most, measurements can certify that a deterministic fixture has a target duration, falls inside its broad centroid band, and meets explicitly stated pairwise numeric inequalities. They cannot support sound/perceptual novelty or quality, production, rights, engine, publish, or promotion conclusions. The declared `.30 s` duration alone exceeds the `.020 s` pairwise threshold for both existing movements; report that duration arm separately and do not represent the additional centroid filter as perceptual evidence. Candidate selection remains pending parent integration and independent replay.
