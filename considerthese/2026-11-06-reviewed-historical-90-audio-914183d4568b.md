# Audio: direction changes perceived motion

[Neuhoff, 1998](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/13542134_Perceptual_bias_for_rising_tones) reports greater perceived loudness change for rising than equivalent falling tones; the corresponding white-noise difference was not significant. Stimulus type matters.

[Neuhoff, 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5256440/) extends the question to speed judgments: looming sounds were perceived as faster than equivalent receding sounds, and proximity also mattered. Participants listened online using their own equipment; relative differences were the main concern, not precise absolute speed estimates.

The earlier experiment remains useful for separating intensity change from physical motion. The later work adds direction and distance to the interpretation of apparent pace, without establishing a universal game-audio adjustment or player-performance benefit. Selected retained results/discussion only; no audio played or experiment reproduced.
