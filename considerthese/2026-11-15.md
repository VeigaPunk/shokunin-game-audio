## Three most important lessons from the window

1. **Absent is not zero, and zero is not silence.** Three of the seven notes make this same point in different formats. In sample-count-slot-migration, a BW64 dummyLow/dummyHigh value of zero (the field that was sampleCountLow/High in RF64 under EBU Tech 3306) does not mean zero samples or silence. In typed-discard-counts, under RFC7002, a discard block that lacks its measurement-interval context must be discarded, and "missing context is not a zero count." In processor-lifetime, an AudioWorklet process() call that returns nothing behaves like false, not like success. Together these give the lane a general rule for interpreting fields: record whether a value was present, defaulted, or structurally reassigned before reading what it says.

2. **Define what you are counting before you compute a statistic over it.** loudness-range-population says EBU Tech 3342 LRA is a 10th-to-95th percentile spread taken after an absolute gate and a relative gate. The relative gate sits 20 LU below the absolute-gated loudness, and it is not the integrated-loudness gate. processor-lifetime says termination should be specified separately from the sample calculation, because empty inputs do not prove that internal state such as a tail is used up. In both notes the main mistake is upstream of the arithmetic: the wrong windows are included, or the wrong lifetime is assumed. This makes the lane's game-audio guidance deeper: effects need an explicit lifecycle owner, and loudness reports need their gating population stated.

3. **Edition dating is now a habit in the notes.** Each note separates the date of the inspected edition from the date of the idea. Web Audio's tail and generator policy is dated to the 2018 CR, with the 2021 REC confirming it. EBU 3342 is described as published in 2010, revised in 2016, with the 2023 edition inspected. For BS.2088-2 (Nov 2025, with 2026 amendments), the note says the date belongs to the inspected edition, not to the field change. Each note also ends with the source's reuse terms (for example, W3C's document license restricts derivatives). The notes now carry provenance alongside their content, which makes later citation safer.

## Self-improvement ideas (proposals only, not acted on)

- **Absence-semantics register.** Keep a small table, drawn from the notes, of fields where present, absent, defaulted and zero mean different things (RF64/BW64 slots, RTCP XR blocks, worklet return values, and so on). Each daily pass would flag any new note that reads a field's value without stating which of those four states it is in. This turns lesson 1 into a check I can repeat.
- **Edition-claim spot check.** Each day, pick one cited source and confirm with a read-only fetch that the stated edition or date matches what the link actually serves (PDF title page or metadata, or the W3C dated URL). Report a match or mismatch with a [fetched] tag. That would show whether the dating habit in lesson 3 holds up when checked against the source.
- **Per-source reuse-terms register.** Collect the license line from every note into one list grouped by issuer (W3C, EBU, ITU, IETF, Xiph, and so on). Then, before João runs any "Proposed practice, unperformed" exercise, I could say up front which terms limit what derived fixtures, traces or hashes can be published. This is a read-only summary and would not give any legal clearance.
