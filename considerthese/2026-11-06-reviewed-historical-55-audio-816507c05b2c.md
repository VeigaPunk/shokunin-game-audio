# Audio: quieter pauses do not certify preservation

[Boll](https://course.ece.cmu.edu/~ece491/homework/Boll79.pdf), April 1979, estimates noise during nonspeech intervals. Background changes can invalidate the estimate. Averaging risks smearing transients; rectification can remove speech. Residual suppression already belongs to this baseline. Helicopter-speech evaluation is not a game-dialogue guarantee.

[Ephraim and Malah](https://malah.net.technion.ac.il/files/2017/08/Ephraim_Speech_Enhancement_ASSP84.pdf), December 1984, models independent Gaussian spectral components. Decision-directed SNR estimation uses previous amplitude estimates; signal-presence uncertainty receives explicit treatment. Stationary added-noise evaluation and informal listening do not establish arbitrary-background performance.

For restoration review, identify protected performance details separately from unwanted background, and preserve the reason for choosing a reference interval. A quieter pause or recognizable transcript alone cannot establish unchanged delivery. Selected primary passages only; no enhanced audio, listening experiment or current product recommendation.
