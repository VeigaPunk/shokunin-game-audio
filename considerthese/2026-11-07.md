1. **Format metadata got deeper: what a file declares is not the same as what the signal is.** Five notes each separate a declared field from the truth underneath it:
   - `channel-mask`: the channel count does not tell you which speaker each channel is. The WAVEFORMATEXTENSIBLE mask does, in set-bit order.
   - `exclusive-loop-endpoint`: in the Web Audio 1.1 Sept 2026 Working Draft, `loopEnd` is exclusive, so the last sample you include is not the endpoint. Positions are in buffer time and can fall between sample frames.
   - `flac-sample-checksum`: the FLAC STREAMINFO MD5 (RFC 9639) hashes the decoded samples, not the file bytes, and a value of zero means unknown.
   - `qoa-slice-tail`: a QOA slice is always 20 samples, padded with zeros, so file size is not the same as duration.
   - `rtp-timestamp-clock`: in RFC 3551, G.722 audio runs at 16000 Hz but its RTP clock runs at 8000 Hz.

   Together they give asset handoff a concrete rule: record layout, loop interval units, the decoded-sample hash and the clock as separate fields.
2. **Coverage widened into perception and synthesis research.** The 18 historical notes cover Karplus-Strong and the Jaffe and Smith extensions (keep tuning and decay specified separately), material perception from impact sounds (Klatzky/Pai/Krotkov 2000, Giordano/McAdams 2006), phonemic restoration, perceived motion and direction, granular populations, antialiasing placement, declipping and interpolation, and clapping. This is the first real coverage in the lane of why sounds read as wood, metal or a moving object, not just how files are stored.
